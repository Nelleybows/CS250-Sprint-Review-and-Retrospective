## Module 8 Reflection

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?
  Understanding user needs is vital to creating a product that solves a problem for the user. This starts with figuring out what the user is trying to accomplish and what problem they need the program to solve. From there, I can use requirements and user stories to break those needs into smaller tasks that can be developed and tested. User stories help keep development focused on what the user actually needs rather than adding features that may not be necessary, while also helping prioritize the most important functions. When something is unclear, I don't hesitate to reach out and get a better understanding of the needed functionality. Making assumptions can lead to undesirable results and waste time and resources. Testing the program against the requirements to make sure they are successfully met is also an important step in implementing user needs. This helps keep development focused on creating something useful instead of just something that works.


### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?
  When developing programs, I approach it by making sure I have a clear understanding of the requirements before writing any lines of code. I keep in mind that project requirements can change over time, so using good syntax and object-oriented programming (OOP) principles can help make code more adaptable to those changes. Once I have a proper understanding of the requirements, I would break them down into smaller pieces that can be understood and developed more quickly and efficiently. When deciding which tasks to start with, I would consider their priority, whether that priority was given to me by another team member or the client, or based on what seems most important to the client's vision. Learning about Agile has shown me that development requires flexibility, especially when projects need to adapt to changes. Feedback throughout the process can also help identify issues earlier rather than waiting until the end to make corrections. In my future development work, I would like to incorporate strong communication, Daily Scrums, and Sprint Retrospectives. I felt communication was a core principle taught throughout Agile, woven into all the efforts of an Agile team. Daily Scrums help keep everyone informed and give team members an opportunity to better understand the project and their teammates. Sprint Retrospectives provide a way to review the completed sprint and identify areas for improvement in the future. I take a similar approach in my current career, using communication to work more efficiently and reviewing my work to identify areas where I can improve. I hope to carry that same mindset into my future development work.


### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?
  Being a good team member in software development means being someone who communicates effectively, can be depended on, and is willing to adapt to different ideas or changes when they occur. A good team player actively listens to their team and shares input when they feel it is helpful. Even though everyone on a team might not share the same responsibilities, each person's work contributes to completing the project collaboratively. Sharing progress, participating in meetings, and addressing issues early rather than ignoring them are all important responsibilities that help a project run smoothly. I also think being open to feedback is important because there is always something new to learn, especially in software development. As I continue working toward becoming a software developer, I want to be someone my team can depend on, who contributes to solving problems and is willing to learn from others.
